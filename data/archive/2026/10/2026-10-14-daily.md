### 6. ChunkAct: Action Chunking for Agentic Robotics
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.RO
- **Summary:** Agentic robotics places a large language model (LLM) agent in the robot control loop, where it composes perception, motion, and vision-language-action (VLA) tools under the guidance of a memory and thereby generalizes to new tasks without retraining. This generality comes at a steep price in efficiency. Every primitive requires a round of LLM reasoning over the full interaction history, so the cost of an episode grows with the number of primitives. We find this reasoning largely redundant: once a sub-goal is chosen, its primitives follow a routine already prescribed by memory. We present ChunkAct, which introduces action chunking to agentic robotics. Online, a single call launches a chunk of chained primitives, which executes autonomously and returns control only upon failure. Offline, ChunkAct compiles the memory into these chunks, so execution is memory-free at test time and the planner is reserved for decisions that require reasoning. LLM computation therefore scales with the number of decisions rather than the number of primitives. On LIBERO-Pro, ChunkAct improves task success by 5.0 points over the state of the art in agentic robotics while requiring 2.9x fewer LLM calls, 4.4x fewer input tokens, and 2.3x fewer output tokens.
- [Open source item](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18874v1)

### 8. Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.CL
- **Summary:** Long-horizon agents have drawn growing attention for their ability to work autonomously -- from hours to days -- on economically valuable tasks, a capability essential to automated discovery and recursive self-improvement (RSI). Yet open-source models still struggle on long-horizon tasks, largely for lack of both training data and a working recipe, and scaling both is hard: long, complex tasks are difficult to curate and verify, and their rewards are sparse. We address both. First, we introduce Tev-Gym, the first long-horizon agent training dataset with verifiable, dense, progressive rewards -- 1,170 verified task environments across 7 domains. ### 10. DoubtLess: Training-Free Contrastive Decoding for Efficient Reasoning
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.CL
- **Summary:** Reasoning models achieve strong performance through long chains of thought (CoT), but often spend more tokens than needed to solve a problem. Existing ways to shorten the CoT require additional training for each model, add a fixed steering vector to the model's hidden states, or use decoding heuristics such as stopping early or banning words like "wait", which can reduce accuracy. We propose DoubtLess, a training-free decoding method in which a much smaller reasoning model steers the large one towards brevity. The small model reads the large model's CoT so far twice, once prompted to be brief (the brief thinker) and once prompted to be verbose (the verbose thinker), and the difference between its two logit vectors, scaled by a strength, is added to the large model's logits at every step of the CoT. On seven benchmarks of competition mathematics, graduate-level science and competitive programming, DoubtLess shortens the CoT of Qwen3-32B by 31% with unchanged accuracy, outperforming five inference-time baselines. We also show that DoubtLess reduces thinking tokens per turn by 20% in a self-debugging coding agent with unchanged task performance. It also generalizes to two other model families with unchanged accuracy. When combined with training-based compression, it further shortens the CoT of a model already trained for brevity. Ablations and behavioral analysis show that DoubtLess reduces repeated checking, with most token savings coming after the correct answer first appears in the CoT. Our code is available at https://github.com/leoluolol/DoubtLess.
