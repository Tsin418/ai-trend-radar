### 6. ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.CV
- **Summary:** Visual agents answer questions about ultra-high-resolution photos by zooming into the relevant regions. However, existing agents often answer from common sense instead of reading the evidence. Reinforcement learning (RL) on the final answer cannot correct this, because a guess earns the same reward as reading. We propose Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization (CrossRPO), which trains agents on contrast sets. A contrast set asks one question about several versions of an image that differ only in a small region with the evidence and have different answers, so only reading the evidence solves the whole set. CrossRPO rewards solving the whole set, and we prove that it does not reinforce a set answered from common sense. We build contrast sets for training (ViRA-Train) and evaluation (ViRA-Bench) by editing one small object in images of 21 to 120 megapixels, and we photograph further sets with real changes (ViRA-Bench-Real) to exclude editing traces. We train two Visual Reasoning Agents, ViRA-9B and ViRA-27B, with CrossRPO on only about 3,500 items of ViRA-Train each. CrossRPO achieves 63.3% set accuracy on ViRA-Bench at the maximum resolution, against 59.3% for GRPO. ViRA-9B is the most accurate open model of its size on all benchmarks, and ViRA-27B outperforms Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6-Luna, both at their highest thinking level, on most benchmarks.
- [Open source item](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21605v1)

### 7. Quantifying the added value of human forecaster discussions for tropical cyclone intensity prediction
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.LG
- **Summary:** The National Hurricane Center routinely releases Tropical Cyclone Discussions that offer insights into its tropical cyclone forecasts. This study examines the added value of these discussions for intensity forecasting beyond a set of numerical predictors. For 637 Atlantic and eastern North Pacific tropical cyclones from the 2002-2023 hurricane seasons, we analyze the discussion text and its combination with 13 hurricane environmental predictors from SHIPS and ERA5 10-meter wind fields, for forecasting intensity changes at lead times of 24 to 120 hours using three modeling methods. In one approach, an end-to-end deep learning framework for the text shows that the discussions alone offer comparable value for 24-hour intensity forecasts to the SHIPS predictors. Adding discussion information to the SHIPS predictors in an ensemble of five such models reduces the mean absolute error of 24-hour maximum sustained wind forecasts for 2022-2023 cyclones from 9.89 to 8.90 kt, with a confidence interval that excludes zero when whole storms are resampled. The ERA5 wind fields have a minor effect on mean absolute error in wind predictions, and model rankings differ across predicted variables, lead times and model types. We provide prediction intervals for these forecasts, whose coverage falls below the 90% target during rapid intensification. Analysis of the text shows that words related to intensity and intensity change (such as intensify, weaken, eyewall and category) are important for the model predictions. This retrospective study shows the added value of the forecasters' discussions and suggests that multi-modal learning can help understand the predictive contribution of expert human judgment. The reanalysis data differ from real-time analyses, and the discussions are issued at least three hours after the valid time of the other inputs, so these results should not be interpreted operationally.

### 8. Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting with Physics-Indexed Specialist Memory: the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.LG
- **Summary:** Continual reinforcement learning requires adaptation to changing conditions while preserving performance under previously encountered dynamics. It was investigated whether specialists generated by evolutionary search can provide a reusable memory when combined with environment detection and failure-driven search. The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) evolves small neural networks without gradients, archives selected policies by detected dynamic regime, and retrieves them through Bayesian policy reuse. The architecture was evaluated in non-stationary CartPole and HalfCheetah environments. ### 9. SADL: On the Demand-Supply Geometry of Representation Collapse
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.RO
- **Summary:** In Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), a well-constrained projector output does not by itself prevent representation collapse. We find that, from initialization, the encoder representation is dominated by a component shared by different inputs. A batch-normalized projector removes this component, so regularization after the projector cannot correct it, and the differences between representations shrink until they collapse. We propose SADL (Supply And Demand Loss), which keeps the original training objective and constrains the encoder directly when needed. SADL compares the representation differences that the projector output demands with the differences the encoder can retain and pass on to the projector, and adds a constraint on the encoder only when supply falls short of demand. It works with different Demand Regularizers and introduces no auxiliary networks, additional trainable parameters, or architectural changes. In single-task and jointly trained four-task LeWorldModel (LeWM) and INTACT experiments, SADL lowers the collapse rate over ten random seeds from 50% to 0%. After one epoch of training, encoder effective rank is 3.4-6.4 times that of the corresponding baseline without SADL, and latent prediction error falls to 1/20-1/303 of it. Without additional tuning, SADL stays robust as its loss weight spans orders of magnitude, and with several Demand Regularizers INTACT exceeds 95% mean search-free control success. On four image datasets, SADL also consistently improves accuracy and stability across random seeds. These results show that matching the representation differences a regularizer demands with the differences the encoder can supply leads to faster and more stable representation learning in control and image classification.
