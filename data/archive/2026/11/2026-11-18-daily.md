### 6. Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.LG
- **Summary:** Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. In our method, a "frozen" version of the pre-trained model serves as a noisy teacher that the fine-tuned model must match at a coarse level. In particular, we require that the high-temperature versions of the conditional distributions produced by the post-training model should match the high-temperature versions of the pre-training ones. The temperature hyperparameter controls the level of trust in the pre-trained model, with the limit $T\to \infty$ reverting back to regular SFT and $T=1$ to knowledge distillation. We demonstrate that our approach outperforms standard SFT across domain adaptation, instruction-tuning, and reasoning, leading for up to $21\%$ average relative improvement to standard SFT.

### 7. Spool: Low-Latency Streaming Inference for Hybrid Vision-Language Models
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** cs.CV
- **Summary:** Applications such as visual accessibility agents and robotics require answering questions about recent video content promptly, over a bounded window of past frames (e.g., the last two minutes). Hybrid vision-language models (VLMs), which interleave attention with linear recurrent layers such as Gated DeltaNet (GDN), are attractive for this setting, but maintaining a sliding window is expensive. The reason is that recurrent layers combine all past frames into a shared state, so expiring old frames normally requires re-running the entire window through the model. We observe that the recurrent update is affine in the state with input-dependent coefficients, so the effect of each frame can be captured as a transformation that composes associatively. We present Spool, a training-free framework that caches per-frame state transformations and composes them efficiently as the window advances, removing expired frames from each recurrent layer's state without replaying frames through the backbone. Removal is exact within each layer; as with key-value eviction in attention, expired frames can retain residual influence through the cached inputs computed by earlier layers. We design a dedicated data structure and GPU kernels to achieve low caching and composition overhead. Across five open-source hybrid VLMs and four video reasoning benchmarks, Spool achieves the lowest p95 time-to-first-token in all 20 model-benchmark tests, reducing it by a geometric mean of $6.6\times$ over the faster of two recent vLLM baselines, while improving accuracy by 2.3 percentage points on average.
- [Open source item](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23228v1)

### 9. An inexact stochastic variance-reduced gradient method for constrained convex finite-sum optimization
- **Source:** arxiv
- **Type:** paper
- **Category:** math.OC
- **Summary:** In this paper, we study a class of constrained convex finite-sum optimization problems. In many practical applications, all or some of the constraints are hard constraints that represent imperative requirements. Consequently, any desirable approximate solution must (nearly) satisfy these constraints. Motivated by this, we focus on computing an $ε$-$surely\ feasible\ stochastic\ optimal$ ($ε$-SFSO) solution, in which the constraint violation is deterministically bounded by $ε$, and the expected optimality gap is at most $ε$. While a recently proposed stochastic variance-reduced first-order penalty method can compute an $ε$-SFSO solution, there remains a significant gap between its first-order oracle complexity for computing an $ε$-SFSO solution and that for computing an $ε$-$expected\ feasible\ stochastic\ optimal$ solution, where both the expected constraint violation and the expected optimality gap are bounded by $ε$. To narrow this gap, we propose an inexact stochastic variance-reduced gradient (SVRG) method that significantly improves the previously best-known first-order oracle complexity for computing an $ε$-SFSO solution. Furthermore, when the objective consists of a single function, the proposed method reduces to a novel deterministic first-order method for solving standard convex conic optimization. It achieves a significantly lower gradient evaluation complexity with respect to the objective function compared to existing methods, and can outperform them when computing constraint gradients and projections onto the associated cone is cheaper than evaluating the objective gradient. Preliminary numerical results are presented to illustrate the performance of the proposed method.

- **Source:** arxiv

- **Summary:** LLM agents increasingly drive their own learning. Self-improvement methods delegate various components of model optimization to the models themselves. In this paper we study an autonomous paradigm in which, given basic primitives, resources, and a general goal for self-improvement, agents construct their own self-learning algorithm for context-based optimization.
